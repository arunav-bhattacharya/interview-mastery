# Common Questions

Almost every behavioral prompt is a re-skin of one of these. Pre-map a story
from your bank to each cluster; you'll never be caught flat-footed.

## Ownership & impact

- Tell me about a project you're most proud of.
- Describe a time you took something on without being asked.
- What's the biggest mistake you've made and how did you handle it?
- Tell me about a time you raised the bar on your team.

## Conflict & collaboration

- Tell me about a disagreement with a peer. How did you resolve it?
- Describe a time you had to push back on a manager.
- How do you give feedback to someone who's resistant to it?
- A time you had to work with someone whose style was different from yours.

## Failure & learning

- Tell me about a time you failed.
- A project that didn't go to plan — what happened?
- A decision you'd make differently in hindsight.
- How do you handle being wrong in a technical discussion?

## Leadership & growth

- A time you led without authority.
- How do you mentor more junior engineers?
- Describe a time you influenced a team-level decision.
- A skill you've developed deliberately in the last year.

## Asking back

When the interviewer says **"do you have questions for me?"** — say yes. A few
that tend to surface useful signal:

- What does a great first ninety days in this role look like?
- What's the team's biggest blocker right now that I'd help unblock?
- How does the team make decisions when there isn't consensus?
- What kind of work does this team find energizing? Draining?

The point is to test fit, not to flatter.
