# The STAR method

<Pillbar pills={[
  { label: 'parts', value: 4 },
  { label: 'budget', value: '~90 seconds' },
]} />

Behavioral interviews aren't trick questions. The interviewer is testing whether
you can **tell a clean story**, and whether the story shows the qualities they
care about (ownership, conflict resolution, technical judgment, etc.).

## The four parts

| Part         | What goes here                                            | Time slice |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| **Situation** | Set the scene in 1–2 sentences. Where, when, who.         | 15s        |
| **Task**     | Your role and the explicit goal.                          | 10s        |
| **Action**   | What **you** did (singular "I", not "we"). Be specific.   | 45s        |
| **Result**   | The outcome — quantified if possible, plus what you learned. | 15s        |

## A worked example

> **Situation** — Late last year, our checkout service started timing out on
> Black Friday traffic. The on-call channel was lighting up at 3am UTC.
>
> **Task** — As the on-call engineer that week, I needed to stabilize the
> service before peak traffic hit Europe a few hours later.
>
> **Action** — I correlated the timeouts with a database hot-key — one popular
> SKU was being read by 30% of cart sessions. I rolled out a read-through Redis
> cache with a 30-second TTL behind a feature flag, validated on a canary, and
> ramped it to 100% over forty minutes.
>
> **Result** — P99 fell from 4.1 seconds to 180 ms. We held through peak with
> headroom. I wrote up the incident and added a hot-key alert to our dashboards;
> we've caught two similar issues since.

## Tips you'll thank yourself for

- **One sentence each for S and T.** Most candidates spend ninety seconds on
  scene-setting and never get to the actions.
- **Use "I"**, not "we". Pre-decide which parts of the group effort were yours.
- **Pick stories with friction** — easy stories are boring stories.
- **Have a number ready** — even a soft one ("cut review time roughly in half").
- **End with a learning** — what you'd do differently next time.

## Build a story bank

A small bank — five stories — covers most prompts. Pick stories that hit at
least one of each:

- A technical decision under ambiguity
- A conflict with a peer or manager
- A failure you owned
- A leadership moment without authority
- A delivery success against a hard deadline

Then for every prompt, ask: **which story in my bank best fits this?** That
mapping is faster than improvising fresh.
