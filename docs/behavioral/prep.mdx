## Topic 1: The STAR Method

Behavioral interviews are not trick questions. The interviewer is testing whether you can **tell a clean story** and whether the story demonstrates qualities they care about (ownership, conflict resolution, technical judgment).

### The four parts

| Part | Goal | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Situation** | Set the scene in 1–2 sentences. Where, when, who. | 15s |
| **Task** | Your role and the explicit goal. | 10s |
| **Action** | What **you** did, specifically. Use "I", not "we". | 45s |
| **Result** | Outcome (quantified if possible) + what you learned. | 15s |

### A worked example

> **Situation** — Last Black Friday our checkout service started timing out, and the on-call channel was lighting up at 3 a.m. UTC.
>
> **Task** — As the on-call engineer that week, I needed to stabilize the service before peak traffic hit Europe a few hours later.
>
> **Action** — I correlated the timeouts with a database hot-key — one popular SKU was being read by 30% of cart sessions. I rolled out a read-through Redis cache with a 30-second TTL behind a feature flag, validated on a canary, and ramped to 100% over forty minutes.
>
> **Result** — P99 fell from 4.1 seconds to 180 ms. We held through peak with headroom. I wrote up the incident and added a hot-key alert to our dashboards; we've caught two similar issues since.

### Why STAR works

- It forces you to **finish** the story (no orphan setups).
- It signals **ownership** (focus on Action).
- It surfaces **quantified impact** (Result).

## Topic 2: Common Behavioral Questions

Almost every prompt is a re-skin of one of these five clusters. Pre-map a story from your bank to each.

### Ownership & impact

- Tell me about a project you're most proud of.
- Describe a time you took on something without being asked.
- What's the biggest mistake you've made and how did you handle it?
- Tell me about a time you raised the bar on your team.

### Conflict & collaboration

- Tell me about a disagreement with a peer. How did you resolve it?
- Describe a time you had to push back on a manager.
- How do you give feedback to someone resistant to it?
- A time you worked with someone whose style was different from yours.

### Failure & learning

- Tell me about a time you failed.
- A project that didn't go to plan — what happened?
- A decision you'd make differently in hindsight.
- How do you handle being wrong in a technical discussion?

### Leadership & growth

- A time you led without authority.
- How do you mentor more junior engineers?
- Describe a time you influenced a team-level decision.
- A skill you've developed deliberately in the last year.

### Ambiguity & prioritization

- A time you had to make a decision with incomplete information.
- How do you decide what to work on when everything seems urgent?
- A trade-off between speed and quality.

## Topic 3: Prepare Your Stories

You only need **8–10 well-prepared stories**. Each story should hit at least one of:

1. A **technical decision** under ambiguity.
2. A **conflict** with a peer or manager.
3. A **failure** you owned.
4. A **leadership moment** without authority.
5. A **delivery success** against a hard deadline.
6. A **scope challenge** — saying no, or saying yes carefully.
7. A **mentoring** moment.
8. An **incident** you led or recovered from.

### Story template (1-pager per story)

```text
TITLE: The one-line headline of the story
WHEN: Quarter / year, team / company
WHO: Roles involved (you, manager, peer, stakeholder)
SITUATION: 1–2 sentences setting the scene
TASK: The explicit goal you owned
ACTION: 4–6 bullets, "I" verbs, technical specifics
RESULT: Quantified outcome + one learning
TAGS: ownership | conflict | failure | leadership | delivery
```

### Practice this drill

For each prompt in the common-questions list, ask: **which of my stories best fits?** That mapping is faster than improvising.

## Topic 4: Interview Tips

### What to do

- **Use "I", not "we".** The interviewer is grading you, not your team.
- **Pick stories with friction.** Easy stories are boring stories.
- **Have a number ready** — even soft ("cut review time roughly in half").
- **End with a learning.** What you'd do differently.
- **Repeat the question** in your own words. Confirms scope, buys thinking time.
- **Pause before answering.** A 2-second silence is fine; rambling is not.

### What to avoid

- **Over-setting-the-scene.** One sentence each for S and T.
- **Vague impact** ("the project went well").
- **Blaming others** for failures. Senior candidates own outcomes.
- **Lying or embellishing.** Interviewers ask follow-ups; they will catch holes.
- **Forgetting to follow up.** "I learned X and applied it on Y next time" is the seal on a story.

### Questions to ask **them**

When the interviewer says *"do you have questions for me?"* — say yes.

- What does a great first 90 days in this role look like?
- What's the team's biggest blocker right now that I'd help unblock?
- How does the team make decisions when there isn't consensus?
- What kind of work does this team find energizing? Draining?
- How is performance measured for this role specifically?

The point is to test fit, not to flatter.

## Mock Interview Practice

You can't get good at behavioral interviews by reading. Practice out loud.

### Solo practice

- Record yourself answering 5 prompts; play it back.
- Time each answer (target: under 2 minutes).
- Listen for filler words ("um", "like", "you know").

### Pair practice

- Trade with another candidate or friend.
- One asks, one answers, then critique.
- Pramp / Interviewing.io for free or low-cost.

### Mock with a senior

- Find a current senior engineer at a target company.
- Their feedback on "what landed" is gold.

## Final Words

Consistency beats intensity. Two focused hours daily > fourteen hours on a weekend.

The same skill compounds across all rounds: **clear, structured, honest communication**. Practice it in DSA rounds, system design, and behavioral; the interviewer's mental model of "is this person senior?" is built from the same signal every time.

Good luck.
